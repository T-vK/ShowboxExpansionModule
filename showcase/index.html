01 — The link

There was no spec. There was a wire.

Every message is framed BE EF … EF BE. Inside it, an entity: a bool, a byte, or a float. Gain, EQ, compressor, looper state, snapshots, the tuner, the battery. The firmware sits in the middle of the conversation. It can answer from a cache of what it has already heard, or inject a packet the other side will accept as its own.

BEEF 080358 00000042 EFBE

That frame is a real shape from the link: entity write, float payload. The class that understands it is the product. Footswitches and MIDI controllers never talk to the Showbox directly. They talk to this.

02 — The system

      
One microcontroller, a lot of edges.

          
The expansion module is a man-in-the-middle with a product wrapped around it. Audio stays on Mackie’s path. Control goes through the module.

      

        

          
Showbox base

          
The powered speaker. It owns the amp, the looper engine, and half of the undocumented UART.

        

        
UART

        

          
Expansion module

          
ESP32-S3. Reads, caches, and writes entities. Serves the UI. Speaks MIDI and HID on whatever port you plugged in.

        

        
UART

        

          
Breakaway Mixer

          
The surface musicians actually touch. The module can impersonate it, follow it, or both.

        

      

      

        
FeetFour TRS jacks. Dual footswitches and expression pedals for looper, mute, boost, snapshots.

        
MIDIDIN, USB device, USB host, AppleMIDI on the W5500, and BLE central plus peripheral.

        
HIDKeyboard, mouse, and a pointer map chosen so Linux and phones both bind the device.

        
The phoneA PWA served by the chip. Captive portal, OTA, and a browser flasher for the firmware itself.

      


      

        

          
Looper, matched to the mixer

          
Record, overdub, play, and undo are not a generic state machine. Press timing follows the gestures the mixer already uses, including a second tap when the first one was missed.

        

        

          
X-Touch Compact

          
A Behringer control surface maps onto channel gain, EQ, mute, and the master. The same entity cache feeds the rings and the faders.

        

        

          
Radios that have to share a chip

          
Wi-Fi, BLE MIDI, and BLE HID come up together. Bonds, the hostname showbox.local, and the HID report map stay stable across reboots.

        

        

          
A mixer UI on the chip

          
Channels, EQ, compressor, effects, the looper, snapshots. The interface is a single-file PWA the device hosts, with a live socket back to the entity cache.

04 — What the work is

      
A product, built from an undocumented wire.

      

        
01

        

          
Protocol, until it has types

          
A C++ model of the link: packet kinds, 94 named entities, and getters that return the bool, byte, or float the hardware last committed to. New features are writes against that model.

        

      

      

        
02

        

          
Firmware that is the product

          
PlatformIO on an ESP32-S3. The control loop, the web server, USB mode switching, Ethernet, and two radios share one chip. When they fight — a BLE disconnect stalling the stack, a bond that forgets the HID map — the fix is in the same tree as the feature.

        

      

      

        
03

        

          
Mechanical design that closes

          
The enclosure is dimensioned around parts you can buy, printed, and photographed in the speaker it was made for. The viewer on this page loads that mesh. Orbit it, open the lid, show the electronics, or cut a section through the wall.

        

      

      

        
04

        

          
Still on the bench

          
A few fields on the wire are still unnamed, and the looper is tuned against the real mixer rather than a document that does not exist. That is the job: make it behave, then make it legible.
